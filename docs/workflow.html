One session, start to finish

The club plays weekly. Everything below happens between the group chat waking up and the last set being logged. Only the clay-topped steps need a person — the rest the app does on its own once it knows who turned up.

Before play
01

Players reply in the group chat

No new app and no new habit for anyone who just wants to turn up and play. The club's existing chat carries on exactly as before.

→
02

Organizer marks who's in

Tap names on the Today tab, or paste the chat thread into the box and let it match names against the directory. Timestamps and banter are filtered out.

→
03

Set the date, time and length

The forecast for that window appears inline — the day's high and low, and the UV across the hours actually being played.

Setting up
04

Choose a format per set

Mixed, split by sex, or singles — one choice per set. Court count and numbering follow from the headcount, starting at court 1.

→
05

Check the real court calendar

A link out to the club's live schedule. The app cannot read it, so this is the one step that stops a lesson and a session landing on the same court.

→
06

Pairings generate

Skill balanced, repeat partners avoided, serving evened across teams, sit-outs shared. Any set can still be adjusted by hand afterwards.

On court
07

Sheet goes back to the chat

One tap copies the whole schedule in readable form, ready to paste where everyone is already looking.

→
08

Winners marked as sets finish

Anyone can log a result — no PIN needed. A streak of three or more earns a flame, and a win against a higher-rated pair is tagged as an upset.

→
09

History updates itself

Every logged result is kept against the session date. Win/loss records are readable by anyone; only an Admin can change them.

Afterwards
10

The sheet ages out

Each browser keeps its own sheet. One that has sat untouched for eight days, with its date already past, is cleared automatically.

→
11

Usage is recorded

Counts and durations only, visible to the Superuser tier, including how many sheets went out without needing a hand adjustment.

→
12

Next week starts clean

Start New Week clears who's playing and this week's pairings. The directory and the full match history are untouched.

What changes week to week, and what doesn't

The most common confusion is which list resets. The week resets. The club does not.

Resets every week

Who's playing

Cleared by Start New Week, or when a sheet ages out after eight idle days.

This week's pairings

Regenerated from scratch each time. Re-rolling is free — nothing is lost by trying again.

Session date, time and length

Set fresh each week. The forecast and the date results are filed under both follow from it.

Set formats

Saved with the week, so last week's choice doesn't silently carry over.

Persists indefinitely

The player directory

Names, ratings, handedness, notes and court preferences. Hand-entered and effectively irreplaceable — which is why an import shows every change before applying it and takes a restorable snapshot first.

Match history

Every logged result, kept against its date. Never touched by anything that clears a week, and never removed by sheet retention.

Access control

The Superuser PIN and every Admin PIN, stored only as salted hashes, in a separate record from the directory.

What it deliberately never holds

No phone numbers, emails or addresses — only what the pairing needs. The app is also kept out of search engines.

How it decides, in plain terms

It tries 250 different arrangements of the same people, scores each one, and keeps the best. Points are lost for:

  • One team being stronger than the other — by far the heaviest penalty
  • Pairing two people who have already partnered today
  • Mixing the fiercely competitive with the purely social on one court
  • One team clearly out-serving the other, or both weak servers on one side
  • Benching someone who has already sat out more than their share

Strength means the club's CTA rating. A USTA rating is used only for players who have no CTA rating. Winning and losing never changes either — ratings are set by hand.

Two sheets at once. Each browser keeps its own weekly sheet, so two organizers can plan the same session without overwriting each other. Taking a copy of someone else's pulls their latest version, hand adjustments included.
Court numbers are not verified. The app assigns them from the headcount alone and cannot see the club calendar, so step 05 is the only thing standing between a printed court number and a lesson already booked on it.
